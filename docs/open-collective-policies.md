## Contribution Policy (~850 characters)

The Caregiver Liberation Fund (CLF) exists to move flexible, direct, and transparent funding to organized bodies of caregivers — Community Health Workers, promoters, auxiliaries, midwives, and other frontline care providers — around the world.

We welcome contributions from individuals, foundations, and institutions who share our belief that caregivers closest to the community are best positioned to decide how funding is used. Contributions support two channels: (1) CLF's general operating and capacity-building work, and (2) direct, purpose-aligned transfers to our partner caregiver organizations, each of whom maintains full autonomy over how funds are used within an agreed eligible-use framework.

We do not accept contributions that come with conditions restricting a caregiver organization's autonomy, or from sources listed in our rejected-categories policy below. All contributions and their allocation are publicly visible on this platform.

## Invoice Expenses Policy (~1,050 characters)

Invoices are for vendors, contractors, and service providers billing CLF or a partner caregiver organization directly for goods or services rendered (e.g., training materials, translation services, platform/software costs, venue rental for a caregiver convening).

To submit an invoice:
- Use a clear, itemized description of the goods/services provided and the dates covered.
- Attach a real invoice or receipt showing the vendor's name, amount, currency, and date.
- Reference which caregiver organization or CLF program the expense supports.
- Payment information must match the legal name on the invoice.

Invoices for amounts outside our normal range for a given caregiver organization's budget, or for uses not already covered by that organization's agreed eligible-use list, should include a short note explaining the purpose — our team may follow up before approval.

## Reimbursement Expenses Policy (~1,150 characters)

Reimbursements are for individuals — caregivers, CLF team members, or partner organization staff — who have already paid out of pocket for something that supports CLF's mission and want to be paid back.

Eligible reimbursements generally include: travel to caregiver convenings or trainings, materials and supplies used directly in caregiver organizing or service delivery, and reasonable per-diem costs tied to CLF-related work.

To submit a reimbursement request:
- Attach the original receipt or proof of payment (must show the amount, date, and what was purchased).
- Briefly describe how the expense supported caregiver work or CLF's programs.
- Submit as close as possible to the date of purchase — we ask that reimbursements be filed within 60 days.

Personal needs unrelated to caregiving work, and expenses without documentation, cannot be reimbursed. If you're unsure whether something qualifies, ask before you spend — we'd rather answer a question up front than decline a request after the fact.

## Grant Expenses Policy (~1,300 characters)

Grants are the core mechanism through which CLF moves flexible, direct funding to organized bodies of caregivers. Grant funds are intended to be used at the recipient organization's discretion within the following eligible-use categories, consistent with our Theory of Change:

- **Organizing** — costs of convening, coordinating, and building collective power among caregivers (meetings, communications, coalition-building).
- **Health implementation** — direct costs of delivering community health work (supplies, transport, training).
- **Personal needs tied to caregiving work** — costs that stabilize caregivers themselves (e.g., stipends, equipment, protective gear) so they can sustain their work without burnout.

Grant recipients are not required to itemize every dollar against a rigid budget line, in keeping with our commitment to flexible, trust-based funding. We do ask for periodic, good-faith documentation showing broadly how funds were used, so we can report honestly to our own donors.

If a recipient organization wants to use grant funds for something outside these three categories, they're welcome to reach out to CLF directly to request approval before spending — we say yes far more often than no.

## Expense Title Policy (~550 characters)

Give each expense a short, specific title that makes sense out of context — someone reviewing our public transaction history should understand roughly what it was for without opening the attachment.

Good: "Training materials — [Organization Name], March convening"
Good: "Reimbursement — transport to CHW training, [City]"
Avoid: "Expense," "Reimbursement," "Misc," or anything with no organization/purpose reference.

If the expense relates to a specific partner organization or campaign, include their name in the title.
